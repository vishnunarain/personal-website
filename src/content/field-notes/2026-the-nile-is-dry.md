*An imagined dispatch from ancient Egypt. Historical inspiration; thoroughly invented office politics.*

At sunrise, Imhotep made a list.

Build the pyramid. Check the irrigation. Attend to a man who had attempted to lift a limestone block with confidence alone. Explain to the Pharaoh, for the third time, that “eternity” was a design ambition, not a maintenance plan.

By breakfast, the Nile had stopped cooperating.

## A small dependency issue

“The pyramid is on schedule,” said the overseer.

“Excellent,” said Imhotep. “Where are the stones?”

“Across the river.”

“And the boats?”

“On the river.”

“And the river?”

A long silence. A very small crocodile walked past.

The plan, drawn with considerable elegance on expensive papyrus, contained a box labelled **transport**. It did not contain a box labelled **what if transport becomes a desert**.

Imhotep added one.

## The trouble with being useful

Being a polymath sounds wonderful until everyone discovers your office.

The builders wanted an architect. The farmers wanted an engineer. The sick wanted a physician. The palace wanted someone to make all of these requests fit on one scroll, preferably without increasing the budget.

“Can you just take a quick look?” was, even then, a dangerous sentence.

He had mistaken being able to do many things for having to do all of them. His reward for solving yesterday’s problems was a larger selection of today’s.

So he put down the scroll and walked to the riverbed.

## Start with the thing that is actually broken

From the palace, the problem was a late pyramid.

From the quarry, it was a mountain of stranded stone.

From the riverbank, it was painfully obvious: the system everyone depended on was missing its most important ingredient.

No amount of polishing the pyramid drawing would bring back the water.

Imhotep gathered the people who knew the channels, the boats, and the ground. They made a smaller plan. Find what could move. Find what could wait. Stop promising that everything would happen at once.

It was less impressive than the original scroll. It was also the first plan that accounted for the river.

## A note in the margin

I like Imhotep as a character because curiosity rarely respects a job description. Building software also means wandering between disciplines: systems, design, people, and the unglamorous detail that makes an idea work.

The trick is not to become the genius holding everything together. It is to make the work understandable enough that other people can help.

Sometimes that starts with a better question. Sometimes it starts with leaving the meeting and looking at the river.

And sometimes it starts by admitting that the pyramid can wait.
